Introduction

The TimerHub stopwatch is a no-nonsense elapsed-time tool for anyone who needs to measure how long something actually takes — a workout set, a recipe step, a debugging session, or a rehearsal run-through. It runs entirely in your browser, keeps accurate time even if you switch tabs, and logs every lap so you can review your splits afterward instead of trying to remember them.

How to Use

Press the center button to start timing. While running, tap the lap button (the icon on the right) to record a split — the clock keeps running and a new row appears at the top of your lap history showing the time since your last lap and the total elapsed time. Tap the center button again to pause, and use the circular reset icon on the left to clear everything and start over. Reset is only available while the stopwatch is paused, so you never accidentally wipe a run in progress.

Features

  • Centisecond-accurate display, powered by a drift-corrected timing engine rather than a naive interval counter
  • Unlimited lap logging with split time and cumulative time for every lap
  • Automatic highlighting of your fastest and slowest laps once you've recorded two or more
  • Optional soft click on every lap so you can keep your eyes off the screen
  • Distraction-free fullscreen mode for presentations, coaching sessions or shared screens
  • Keeps counting accurately even if you switch tabs or your laptop sleeps briefly

Use Cases

Sports training. Time sprints, rest intervals or drill sets and use the lap list to compare each rep at a glance.

Cooking. Track how long a sauce actually needs to reduce or dough needs to proof, lap by lap, across multiple attempts.

Task duration tracking. Start the stopwatch when you begin a task and lap it every time you switch context, giving you an honest record of where your time went.

Coding and debugging sessions. Lap each time you reproduce a bug or complete a test run to see whether your fixes are actually getting faster.

Public speaking practice. Lap at the end of each section of a talk to check your pacing against a rehearsal script.

Tips

Laps are ordered most-recent-first so the number you care about right now is always at the top — the row labelled "1" at the bottom of the list is always your very first lap. If you're comparing consistency rather than chasing a single fast time, look at how tightly your splits cluster rather than only at the best/worst highlights. Turn off lap sound in noisy or quiet environments using the speaker icon in the top-right corner of the stopwatch.
