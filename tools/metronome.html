Introduction

A metronome is one of the oldest practice tools in music, and one of the least forgiving — a click that drifts or stutters is worse than no click at all. TimerHub's Metronome is built around a lookahead-style audio scheduler rather than a simple on-screen timer, so the beat stays steady at everything from a slow 30 BPM warm-up to a 250 BPM technical run, with a visible pulse and accented downbeat to match.

How to Use

Set your tempo by typing a BPM value directly, using the plus and minus buttons for fine adjustments, or tapping the "Tap Tempo" button in rhythm — after a few taps it calculates your BPM automatically. Choose a time signature to control how many beats are in each measure and which beat is accented. Press the center play button to start the click, adjust the volume slider at any time, and switch to fullscreen for a large, glanceable pulse display in a practice room or on a music stand.

Features

  • Tempo range from 30 to 250 BPM with direct entry, +/- stepping and tap tempo
  • Tap tempo averages your last several taps for a stable, accurate reading
  • Four time signatures — 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and 6/8 — with an accented first beat
  • Web Audio API scheduling for a click that stays responsive under load
  • Large visual pulse and per-beat dot indicators, so you can practice with the sound off
  • Independent volume control separate from your device's system volume

Use Cases

Instrument practice. Lock in timing on scales, etudes and repertoire at any tempo. Band rehearsal. Give the whole group a shared, audible tempo reference during a run-through. Tempo training. Gradually increase BPM on a difficult passage as your accuracy improves. Dance practice. Count choreography against a steady, accented beat. Marching band. Drill step timing and cadence changes against a fixed click before taking it to the field.

Tips

Practice slower than your performance tempo first — accuracy at a reduced BPM transfers to speed far more reliably than the other way around. Use the time signature's accented first beat to internalize where "one" falls in a measure, especially in less familiar meters like 6/8. If you're setting a tempo from a recording or a piece of sheet music, tap along with it a few times using Tap Tempo rather than guessing — it's usually more accurate than typing a number from memory.
