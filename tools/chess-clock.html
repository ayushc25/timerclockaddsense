Introduction

A chess clock only has one job — keep each player's thinking time honest — but it's surprisingly hard to get right on a screen. TimerHub's Chess Clock is built the way a real tournament clock works: two independent countdowns, one tap to end your turn and start your opponent's, and a clear, unmissable signal for whose move it is. There's nothing to install and nothing to configure beyond picking a time control, so you can go from "let's play" to first move in a few seconds.

How to Use

Choose a time control from the presets — Bullet, Blitz, Rapid or Classical — or set your own starting minutes and increment, then press Start Game. Player 1's clock begins running first. After making a move, the player whose turn it is taps their own clock — not their opponent's — which stops their time, adds any increment they've earned, and starts the other player's clock. Repeat for the whole game. Use Pause at any point to freeze both clocks (handy for a rules dispute or a bathroom break), and Reset to return to setup for a new time control.

Features

  • Two fully independent, drift-corrected countdowns — one per player
  • Large tap targets — the entire player panel is the button, ideal for quick taps mid-game
  • Fischer increment support, added to your own clock the instant you end your turn
  • Six standard time-control presets plus fully custom starting time and increment
  • Clear text label — "Your Turn" vs "Waiting" — so the active player is never ambiguous, even at a glance
  • Automatic flag detection with a chime and on-screen notice the moment a player runs out
  • Fullscreen mode for a clean, distraction-free display across the board

Use Cases

Chess. The obvious one — any time control from bullet to classical, at home or at a casual club night. Checkers and Scrabble. Any two-player board game with a turn structure benefits from a visible time budget. Debate timing. Give each side of a structured debate an equal, enforced speaking budget. Board game turn timing. Keep slower players moving in strategy games without a full timer for every phase. Two-player quiz formats. Head-to-head trivia nights where each contestant gets a fixed thinking budget per round.

Tips

Increment (also called "Fischer time") adds a fixed number of seconds to your own clock every time you complete a move — it rewards fast, decisive play without letting a whole game hinge on the last few seconds. A "5+3" time control, for example, gives each player 5 starting minutes plus 3 extra seconds after every move they make. If you're new to timed games, start with a longer control like 15+10 or 30 minutes with no increment — bullet and blitz controls reward pattern recognition more than deep calculation, and they take practice to enjoy. Always double-check the starting time and increment before tapping Start Game — once the game begins, both are locked until you reset.
